DESTINATION GUIDE: Barcelona, Spain

VISA & ENTRY
Spain is an integral part of the European Schengen Area. Citizens of European Union and EEA nations, as well as Switzerland, enjoy freedom of movement and need only a valid national ID card or passport. Passport holders from the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand can enter visa-free for short-term tourism or business stays of up to 90 days within any rolling 180-day period. Your passport must be valid for at least three months beyond your planned date of departure from the Schengen area and issued within the previous 10 years. Non-exempt nationalities must secure a Schengen Short-Stay Visa prior to arrival.

BEST TIME TO VISIT
The ideal times to experience Barcelona are during late spring (May to June) and early autumn (September to October), when daytime temperatures comfortably hover between 20°C and 25°C (68°F–77°F), making city walking tours and Mediterranean beach lounging delightful without the peak summer swelter. July and August bring high heat, heavy humidity, and massive tourist influxes, while many local shops close for holidays in August. Winter from December to February is mild and sunny with highs around 14°C–16°C (57°F–61°F), offering crisp weather and far fewer queues at famous monuments like the Sagrada Família.

LOCAL CUSTOMS
Catalonia boasts a distinct culture and heritage; both Catalan and Spanish (Castilian) are official languages, and locals deeply appreciate visitors who recognize Catalan identity. Dining schedules run significantly later than in northern Europe and America: lunch (the main meal) is typically served between 1:30 PM and 3:30 PM, while dinner rarely begins before 9:00 PM or 10:00 PM. Tipping is modest and not mandatory; leaving 5% to 10% in coins for attentive table service is customary. When entering churches such as the Cathedral of Barcelona or Sagrada Família, dress respectfully with covered shoulders and thighs.

PACKING TIPS
Pack smart-casual European-style clothing with comfortable walking shoes, as Barcelona is best explored on foot across cobblestone streets in the Gothic Quarter and wide avenues in Eixample. In spring and autumn, pack versatile layers, including a light denim or leather jacket, cardigans, and breathable cotton tops. For summer visits, bring lightweight linen shirts, shorts, swimwear, and beach towels for Barceloneta or Bogatell beach, accompanied by high-SPF sunscreen and UV-protective sunglasses. In winter, a medium-weight coat, wool scarf, and warm trousers will keep you comfortable during cool evenings. Most importantly, carry a secure anti-theft cross-body bag or money belt with zip locks to deter pickpockets.

SAFETY & HEALTH
Barcelona is a generally safe metropolitan city with low violent crime rates, but it is notorious for highly organized pickpocketing and bag-snatching rings. High-risk zones include Las Ramblas, Plaça de Catalunya, the Gothic Quarter, Barceloneta beach, and crowded metro stations like Sagrada Família and Sants. Never place your smartphone or wallet on an outdoor cafe table, never leave bags hanging on the backs of chairs, and remain alert to distraction techniques like unsolicited petitions or spilled liquids. Tap water in Barcelona is safe to drink though somewhat chlorinated; bottled or filtered water is widely preferred for taste. Emergency medical assistance is readily accessible via the EU-wide emergency number 112.
