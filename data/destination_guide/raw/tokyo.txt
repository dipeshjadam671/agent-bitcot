DESTINATION GUIDE: Tokyo, Japan

VISA & ENTRY
Japan grants visa exemptions to citizens of nearly 70 nations, including the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand, and most European countries, allowing visa-free stays for short-term tourism up to 90 days. Travelers must hold a valid passport valid for the duration of stay and an onward or return ticket. Japan requires foreign visitors to submit arrival biometric data (fingerprint scans and facial photographs) at immigration. Prior to departure, travelers are strongly advised to complete immigration and customs declarations digitally via the official 'Visit Japan Web' service to generate expedited QR codes for airport arrival clearance.

BEST TIME TO VISIT
Spring (late March to mid-April) and autumn (mid-October to late November) are the most popular and picturesque seasons to visit Tokyo. Spring showcases ethereal cherry blossoms (sakura) with pleasant daytime temperatures around 15°C–20°C (59°F–68°F), though parks and hotels are heavily booked. Autumn offers brilliant crimson and golden foliage (koyo), clear blue skies, and comfortable temperatures averaging 15°C–21°C (59°F–70°F). Summers (June to August) are hot, humid, and rainy with temperatures reaching 33°C (91°F) and typhoons occasionally passing in late summer. Winter (December to February) is chilly, sunny, and dry with daytime highs of 9°C–12°C (48°F–54°F) and rare light snowfalls, providing stunning crisp views of Mount Fuji from Tokyo observation decks.

LOCAL CUSTOMS
Japanese society places great value on cleanliness, consideration for others (omotenashi), and social harmony. Tipping is not practiced in Japan and can be considered confusing or awkward; exceptional service is considered standard. On escalators in Tokyo, always stand on the left and leave the right side open for people walking up. Avoid talking loudly on cell phones or eating while walking on public transport or streets; dispose of trash in sorting bins typically found beside vending machines or convenience stores (7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart). Bowing is the standard greeting; a slight head bow is courteous and welcomed. Remove shoes when entering Japanese homes, traditional ryokan inns, temples, and tatami-mat restaurants.

PACKING TIPS
Tokyo involves tremendous amounts of walking through massive train stations, subway platforms, and expansive neighborhoods like Shibuya and Shinjuku, so bringing well-cushioned, broken-in walking shoes is paramount. Because shoes must be frequently slipped off at temples, traditional eateries, and fitting rooms, choose footwear that is easy to remove and wear clean, intact socks. In winter (December–February), pack warm layers, a thermal heat-tech base layer, a heavy coat or puffer jacket, gloves, and a scarf for cold outdoor winds, noting that indoor trains and department stores are heavily heated. In summer, pack ultra-light breathable clothing, a UV-blocking parasol, a small hand towel or handkerchief to wipe perspiration (restrooms rarely provide paper hand towels), and a portable fan. Include a universal plug adapter (Type A, two-flat-prong, ungrounded) and a pocket mobile Wi-Fi or eSIM for navigating the intricate Tokyo transit system.

SAFETY & HEALTH
Tokyo is widely recognized as one of the safest metropolitan cities in the world, with remarkably low violent and property crime rates. Lost items left on trains or in restaurants are routinely turned into local police boxes (koban) and safely returned. However, basic urban awareness is advised in nightlife districts such as Kabukicho (Shinjuku) and Roppongi, where touts aggressively promote overpriced bars or adult entertainment clubs—never follow street promoters into unknown bars. Tap water across Tokyo meets rigorous safety standards and is completely potable and delicious to drink. Japan has world-class medical facilities, though foreign language translation may be limited outside major international hospitals; always purchase comprehensive travel health insurance before departure.
