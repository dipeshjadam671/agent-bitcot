DESTINATION GUIDE: Reykjavik, Iceland

VISA & ENTRY
Iceland is a member of the European Schengen Area, although it is not part of the European Union. Citizens of EU/EEA countries can enter with a valid passport or national identity card without border controls. Citizens of the US, Canada, the UK, Australia, and New Zealand may visit Iceland visa-free for tourism up to 90 days within any 180-day period. Passports must have at least three months of validity remaining beyond the intended date of departure from the Schengen zone. Visitors may occasionally be required to provide evidence of accommodation bookings, return tickets, and proof of sufficient funds to cover their stay in Iceland.

BEST TIME TO VISIT
The best time to visit Reykjavik depends heavily on your travel goals. For long daylight hours (up to 24 hours of daylight during the Midnight Sun), accessible highland F-roads, and puffin watching, visit during high summer from mid-June through August, with daytime temperatures averaging 10°C to 15°C (50°F–59°F). For witnessing the magical Northern Lights (Aurora Borealis) and exploring ice caves, plan your trip between late September and late March, when nights are dark and crisp. Winter temperatures in Reykjavik are moderated by the Gulf Stream, averaging around -1°C to 3°C (30°F–37°F), though severe wind gusts and sudden snowstorms can make conditions feel drastically colder.

LOCAL CUSTOMS
Icelanders place immense cultural value on cleanliness, community, and respect for nature. Geothermal swimming pools and hot tubs (heitu pottarnir) are the heart of Icelandic social life; strict pool etiquette requires stripping completely naked and washing thoroughly with soap in communal showers before putting on swimwear and entering the water. Iceland is nearly a cashless society—virtually all vendors, even remote public toilets, accept credit or debit cards and contactless mobile payments. Tipping is not customary in Iceland, as service charges and taxes are already built into all published menu prices. Avoid walking off marked paths or trampling delicate subarctic moss, which takes decades to recover from human disturbance.

PACKING TIPS
The golden rule of packing for Iceland in any season is dressing in modular, weather-resistant layers. Pack thermal base layers (merino wool or high-performance synthetic tops and long johns), an insulating mid-layer (fleece jacket or lightweight down sweater), and a top outer shell that is 100% waterproof and windproof with taped seams (umbrellas are completely useless in violent Icelandic winds). Sturdy, waterproof ankle-high hiking boots with deep tread are indispensable for walking on gravel paths, wet rocks, and volcanic dirt. Bring a thermal beanie, wind-resistant gloves, a wool neck gaiter, and swimwear plus a quick-drying travel towel for geothermal hot springs like the Blue Lagoon or Sky Lagoon. For winter, pack portable microspikes (shoe ice grips) to prevent slipping on icy city sidewalks.

SAFETY & HEALTH
Iceland is consistently ranked among the safest, most peaceful countries in the world, with virtually no violent crime and negligible petty theft. The single greatest hazard facing travelers is the volatile, rapidly shifting subarctic weather and treacherous natural terrain. Always check the official weather website (vedur.is) and road safety alerts (safetravel.is) multiple times daily before traveling outside Reykjavik. Never turn your back on sneaker waves at Reynisfjara black sand beach, stay clear of steaming geothermal vents with scalding water, and never venture onto glaciers without certified mountain guides and safety gear. Tap water throughout Iceland is pure, untreated glacial spring water of the highest quality—always carry a refillable water bottle and drink directly from the tap.
