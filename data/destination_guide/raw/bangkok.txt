DESTINATION GUIDE: Bangkok, Thailand

VISA & ENTRY
Citizens from over 60 countries, including the US, UK, Canada, Australia, and EU member states, can enter Thailand without a visa under the visa exemption scheme for tourism stays up to 30 days. Passports must have at least six months of validity remaining from the arrival date and at least two blank pages. Travelers may be asked to show proof of onward travel and sufficient financial funds (typically 10,000 THB per person). For longer stays, apply for a 60-day tourist visa at a Royal Thai Embassy or online via the official Thailand eVisa portal.

BEST TIME TO VISIT
The best time to visit Bangkok is during the cool and dry season from November through February, when temperatures average between 22°C (72°F) and 31°C (88°F) with lower humidity and minimal rainfall. March through May brings intense tropical heat exceeding 35°C (95°F), leading into the vibrant Songkran Thai New Year festival in mid-April. The monsoon rainy season spans June through October, featuring short, torrential afternoon downpours and humid conditions, though mornings are often clear and tourist crowds are significantly smaller.

LOCAL CUSTOMS
Show utmost respect for the Thai Royal Family and Buddhist traditions; speaking disrespectfully about the monarchy is strictly illegal under strict lèse-majesté laws. When visiting temples (wats) such as Wat Phra Kaew or Wat Arun, wear modest attire that covers both shoulders and knees; remove shoes before stepping onto sacred floors. Use the traditional 'wai' greeting (palms pressed together near the chest with a slight bow) as a sign of respect. Never touch someone's head, as it is considered the most sacred part of the body, and never point the soles of your feet at people or sacred Buddha images.

PACKING TIPS
Pack lightweight, breathable natural fabrics such as linen and quick-dry cotton to cope with high tropical heat and relentless humidity. Include at least one modest outfit covering shoulders and knees for temple visits, along with slip-on shoes or comfortable sandals that are easy to remove at temple entrances. Essential accessories include a compact travel umbrella or lightweight rain poncho for sudden tropical downpours, polarized sunglasses, high-SPF reef-safe sunscreen, insect repellent with DEET, and a reusable insulated water bottle.

SAFETY & HEALTH
Bangkok is generally a very safe destination for tourists, but petty theft, pickpocketing in crowded night markets, and common street scams (such as drivers claiming temples are 'closed' to redirect you to gem or tailor shops) do occur. Always insist that metered taxis use the meter or use ride-hailing apps like Grab. Only drink bottled or filtered water and avoid raw tap water. Street food is world-renowned and generally safe if ordered from busy stalls with high turnover where food is cooked fresh in front of you. Comprehensive travel insurance covering medical evacuation and routine care is strongly recommended.
